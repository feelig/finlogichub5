New Hampshire

New Hampshire annual report and annual fee deadline

Use this page if you need the recurring New Hampshire filing rule. Start by separating the annual report or annual fee that most business entities file by April 1 from the nonprofit report, which runs on a five-year December 31 cycle.

At a glance

Main annual lane Corporations, LLCs, PLLCs, consumer cooperatives, and business trusts use an annual report or annual fee due by April 1 each year following registration.
Published late fee New Hampshire says a $50 late fee is assessed for annual reports and fees received after April 1.
Status consequence Domestic entities fall out of good standing after the miss and can be administratively dissolved after repeated non-filing, while foreign entities can be administratively suspended and nonprofits use a separate five-year report cycle.

Pick your case first

Start with the entity type that matches your record

Use the matching case below before you rely on a fee, filing path, or due date.

Corporation or LLC Most New Hampshire businesses use the April 1 annual lane

The state's online guidance says the annual report and filing fee for many business entity types is due by April 1 each year following registration.

No-change filing One-click filing is available when the record has not changed

NH QuickStart offers a one-click annual report path when there are no business or principal-information changes to update.

Nonprofit report Nonprofits use a different cycle entirely

Domestic and foreign nonprofits file a nonprofit report and fee due by December 31 every five years, not the annual April 1 business-entity lane.

Already late New Hampshire does not let you waive the late fee

The state says the late fee is not waived, skipped years must be cleared in order, and suspended or dissolved records should be handled before you assume the normal filing answer is enough.

Quick questions

What most filers need to know first

These answers keep the page short while still covering the questions people ask before they file.

Question Who should use this New Hampshire guide?

Use this page for Corporations, LLCs, PLLCs, business trusts, and domestic or foreign nonprofits. Match the entity type on the official state record before you rely on the summary.

Question What due date should I start with for New Hampshire?

The main due date on this page is April 1 each year for many business entities, or December 31 every five years for nonprofits. If the state uses anniversary, report-month, or fiscal-year timing, the official state record still controls.

Question What fee or amount does this page show?

The main amount on this page is Common $100 annual report or annual fee lane for many standard business entities, with the late fee added after April 1. If the amount changes by entity type, filing method, or status, confirm it on the official state source before paying.

Question What if I am already late?

$50 late fee after April 1, then not-in-good-standing, dissolution, or suspension risk. Confirm the current status and amount on the official state record before you pay or file.
